The Project Mission

International student-athletes are often seen only for their perfomance on the field/court but behind every jersey is a teenager navigating a massive life transition. My research explores the silent challenges of moving across the world, from the heartbreak of missing family milestones to the stress of maintaining a high GPA in a second/third language.

This website serves as a resource and a voice for those athletes. By sharing real stories and data, I hope to help schools, coaches, and families better support the next generation of international students as they chase their dreams far from home.

Beyond just identifying problems, this project aims to bridge the gap between international athletes and their new communities. Through awareness and shared experiences, we can create a Culture where student-athlets do not just survive their time abroad, but truly thrive, both in their sport and in their personal lives.

Key Finding: According to Goyen (2023), relationships with teammates are just as important as language or money. A supportive coach can prevent an athlete from quitting.

Key Challenges

Navigating life as an international student-athlete means balancing a high-pressure sports career with the hidden emotional and academic hurdles of living far from home.

Mental Health & Homesickness

The emotional weight of being thousand of miles from home while trying to perform at a high level. Manu athletes struggle with lonileness and the pressure to 'stay strong' for their team and family.

Academic Pressure

International students must maintain a high GPA to stay eligible for sports. Balancing 8 hours of classes with 3-5 hours of practice creates a massive time-management challenge.

Culture Shock

Adjusting to new food, different slang, and familier classroom rules. Simple things like how to talk to a coach or a teacher can feel completely different in a new country. Research from Marshall University shows that not understanding locker room humor or idioms is an 'invisible barrier' that makes athletes stay quiet and feel lonely.

Language Barriers

Even for students who speak English, the fast-paced environment and sports specific terminology can be overwhelming. This barrier can lead to misunderstandings on and off field

Athletic Training Load

The intensity of American high school sports is often higher than home. The physical toll of daily training, travel for games, and recovery is constant struggle for students -athletes.

Distance from Family

Dealing with time-zone differences makes it hard to call home. When things go wrong, not having a parent or sibling nearby to talk to can make the journey feel very isolated.

Athlete Survival Guide

  • Language: Practice eye contact and speaking up (American coaches expect this!).
  • Social: Do not be afraid to ask about slang or jokes you don't understand.
  • Support: Find one teammate you trust to help with rides and advice.
